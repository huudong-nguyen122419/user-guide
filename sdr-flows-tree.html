SDR Guideline — by priority

The same ten flows as the task-flow guide, re-ordered by how much of an SDR's day each one takes, with the critical path inside each flow marked so a reader knows what to read first and a fixer knows what to fix first.

Draft v1 · a second version of the SDR guide, kept beside the current one. Nothing here replaces SDR Guideline until you say so.

Q1Which flow does an SDR care about most?

Flow 4 · View & answer replies. It is the only flow where the work turns into an outcome. Everything before it is preparation: a list is not a reply, a send is not a reply. An SDR who reads one screen a day reads this one, and a reply left unanswered is the most expensive thing that can go wrong in the whole guide.

Highest leverage per hour is different: Flow 6 · Manage a campaign. One hour spent there reaches a hundred people; one hour in Flow 3 reaches ten. Care and volume are not the same question, which is why the ordering below separates them.

Q2Which flow is traversed most?

Not one flow, a loop of four, run several times a day:

2 Find a target contact  →  3 Send a 1:1 email  →  4 View & answer replies  →  5 Follow up
                              ↑                                                    │
                              └────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Flow 8 · Using filters is traversed just as often, but never on its own: it runs inside 2, 6 and 7. That makes it a reference, not a step, and it is why a broken filter shows up as three broken flows.

Q3Inside each flow, which part matters most?

The critical path: the nodes every reader passes through and cannot route around. It is marked P0 below. A defect there stops the flow. Everything else is a branch: P1 common with a way around it, P2 genuinely rare. Depth in the tree does not decide priority — reach × blast radius does.

Tier 1 · the daily loop

1Flow 4 · View & answer replies

L0  4  View & answer replies
│
├── L1  4.1  Open the Inbox                          P0  100%   every session starts here
├── L1  4.2  Read the reply, and the chip on it      P0  100%   the chip is a reading, not a decision
├── L1  4.3  Answer it                               P0   ~70%   the outcome
│   └── L2  4.3.1  Reply arrow → prefilled composer
├── L1  4.4  Set the lifecycle                       P0  100%   three labels stop all future sending
└── L1  4.x  Branches
    ├── L2  hot reply → book the call (Flow 4b)      P1
    ├── L2  out of office → leave it                 P1
    └── L2  bounced / dropped → reach another way    P2

Most important part: 4.4, setting the lifecycle. Not because it is hard, because it is the one step with consequences outside this flow. Mislabel an opt-out and every later campaign step still mails them. Answering a reply badly costs one contact; labelling it badly costs every future send.

L0  2  Find a target contact
│
├── L1  2.1  Open your assigned list                 P0  100%
│   └── L2  2.1.1  list not assigned to you          P2
├── L1  2.2  View the contacts                       P0  100%
│   ├── L2  2.2.1  Filter                            P0   ~70%   see Flow 8
│   │   ├── L3  by Lifecycles
│   │   ├── L3  keyword search
│   │   └── L3  has signal
│   ├── L2  2.2.2  Sort                              P1   ~40%

│       ├── L3  show / hide columns

└── L1  2.3  Pick & read a contact                   P0  100%
    ├── L2  2.3.1  open company detail               P1
    └── L2  2.3.2  "+N" more roles                   P2

Most important part: 2.2.1, the filter. The three P0 steps are unavoidable, but the filter is where the day is actually won or lost — it decides who gets contacted. It is also the node with the widest blast radius in the guide: it runs inside Flow 2, Flow 6 and Flow 7.

3Flow 3 · Send a 1:1 email

L0  3  Send a 1:1 email
│
├── L1  3.1  Get into the composer                   P0  100%
│   ├── L2  3.1.1  from the list row ✉               P0   most used
│   ├── L2  3.1.2  from the contact profile          P1
│   ├── L2  3.1.3  from Conversations                P1   history first
│   └── L2  3.1.4  from a signal                     P2   draft written for you
├── L1  3.2  Write it                                P0  100%
│   ├── L2  snippets / template
│   └── L2  attachment
├── L1  3.3  Send                                    P0  100%
└── L1  3.x  Branches
    └── L2  WhatsApp / LinkedIn instead              P2

Most important part: 3.1.1, the ✉ on the list row. Four doors lead to the same composer and one of them carries almost all the traffic. Document that one as the path and the other three as alternatives, rather than presenting four equal options to someone who just wants to write an email.

4Flow 5 · Follow up on silent prospects

L0  5  Follow up on silent prospects
│
├── L1  5.1  Find who never answered                 P0  100%   a filter, again
├── L1  5.2  Decide it is worth a second touch       P0  100%
└── L1  5.3  Send the follow-up                      P0  100%   Flow 3 from here

Most important part: 5.1. The whole flow is a filter plus Flow 3. If the "no reply yet" query is wrong, the SDR either chases people who already answered — which reads as careless — or misses the ones who did not.

5Flow 4b · Schedule a call

L0  4b  Schedule a call
│
├── L1  4b.1  From an interested reply               P0
├── L1  4b.2  Propose / confirm the time             P0
└── L1  4b.3  Set the lifecycle to Call Scheduled    P0   otherwise sending continues

Most important part: 4b.3. The meeting is booked in someone's calendar, but the campaign does not know that until the lifecycle says so. Skip it and a prospect with a meeting on Thursday still receives step 3 on Wednesday.

Tier 2 · bulk work, in bursts

6Flow 6 · Manage a campaign

L0  6  Manage a campaign
│
├── L1  6.1  Create it                               P0
├── L1  6.2  Build the sequence                      P0
│   ├── L2  create a step
│   ├── L2  A/B variants                             P2
│   └── L2  sidestep (conditional)                   P2
├── L1  6.3  Read the list                           P1
├── L1  6.4  Add people                              P0
│   └── L1  6.4b  Remove people                      P1
├── L1  6.5  Preview, personalize & review           P0  ← the gate
│   ├── L2  6.5.1  pick a contact
│   ├── L2  6.5.2  read each step
│   ├── L2  6.5.3  skip a step                       P1
│   ├── L2  6.5.4  personalize + save                P0
│   ├── L2  6.5.5  mark done ✓                       P0   nothing sends without it
│   └── L2  clear out blocked contacts               P1
├── L1  6.6  Hand it over to be run                  P0
├── L1  6.7  The Email Queue                         P1   admin-side
├── L1  6.8  Verify: Inbox & replies                 P0
├── L1  6.9  Label the replies                       P0
└── L1  6.10 Finish the campaign                     P2   admin only

Most important part: 6.5, and inside it 6.5.5 Mark done. It is the last point where a mistake is still free. After the hand-over the emails are real. It is also the one node with a hard mechanical consequence people miss: an unreviewed contact is simply not sent to, silently, so a half-reviewed campaign under-sends and nothing on screen says why.

Tier 3 · foundation and lookup

8Flow 1 · Log in & find your workspace

L0  1  Log in & find your workspace
├── L1  1.1  Log in                                  P0   blocks all ten flows
└── L1  1.2  Find your way around                    P1   read once, then never

Lowest reading value, highest blast radius. Read once on day one; but if it breaks, nobody works. That combination is exactly why depth in the tree cannot be the priority score.

9Flow 8 · Using filters

L0  8  Using filters   — reference, runs inside 2 · 6 · 7
├── L1  include ✓ / exclude ✕                        P0
├── L1  Lifecycles                                   P0
├── L1  Has signal · keyword · countries · cities     P1
└── L1  Simple vs Advanced                           P2

Never walked end to end, so it has no critical path of its own. It inherits the priority of whatever flow is using it — which is why a filter defect is always at least P1.

10Flow 9 · FAQ & edge-case index

A lookup surface, not a flow. In the tree model this becomes an auto-generated index of every branch node sorted by priority, each one showing its parent, rather than a hand-kept list.
