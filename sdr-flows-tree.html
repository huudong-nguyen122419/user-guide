▸A day starts with one question: who do I start with?

An SDR does not open a flow, they open a queue. Every contact on a list is in exactly one of three states, and each state is a different piece of work. Measured on Contact List – Corporates & PortCos, 158 members.

QueueSizeBuilt withWork
Q2Never contacted109 · 69% no filter exists — sort Last Contact and read the empties send the first email → Flow 3
Q3Sent, gone quiet42 · 27% In Conversation : No reply

Q1Replied, waiting on me7 · 4% In Conversation : Received a reply answer → Flow 4

The biggest queue is the one with no name. 109 of 158 people — the group that takes most of the day — cannot be called up by any filter. In Conversation only applies to people who have already been emailed, so anyone never contacted falls into neither Yes nor No, and Last Email Sent Date offers only From / To, never empty.

The workaround is to sort by Last Contact and read where the values run out. That is also the reason the sort exists: it is standing in for a missing filter, not a convenience. One extra value — Not contacted — would give the largest queue a name.

Everything below is that triage, expanded. Do one queue at a time in batch; open a single contact only inside a queue, once you already know what you are doing with them.

To get what
The touch that starts a conversation. Highest volume in the guide — everything upstream exists to make this one email worth opening.
When
Working queue Q2. Which is the queue you cannot filter for, so in practice: sort by Last Contact, work from the empty end down.
Four doors, one room
All four reach the same composer and one carries the traffic. Read 3.1.1 as the path; the others are alternatives. Use 3.1.3 when the contact has history — opening from Conversations means you read what was already said, which is the difference between a follow-up and a duplicate.

3Flow 2 · Find a target contact · feeds Q2 and Q3

L1  2.1  Open your assigned list             P0
     └ list not assigned to you              P2
L1  2.2  View the contacts                   P0
     ├ Filter                                P0   ← builds the queues
     ├ Sort                                  P0   ← the only way to reach Q2
     └ Change the view                       P2
L1  2.3  Pick & read a contact               P0
     ├ View company detail                   P1
     └ “+N” more roles                       P2
To get what
Decides who gets contacted today. Every other flow inherits that decision.
Sort is P0 here, not P1
Corrected after measurement. Sort is not a convenience: with no “never contacted” filter, sorting Last Contact is the only way to reach the 109-person queue.
Why only three columns sort
Only Country · City · Last Contact are sortable, and only those set an order of work. Last Contact turns the list into a queue so nobody at the bottom of page 8 is forgotten. Country / City group by timezone and language, for local-hour batches and sane call times. Title or Company sorted A→Z leads to no decision — their absence is not a gap.
View company detail — when it is worth the click
Three cases only. Before a 1:1, to find one true personal line (Flow 3). On a vague reply, to see who this is and whether a deal is live (Flow 4). While targeting, to check sponsor / portfolio or an existing client so nobody is pitched twice (Flow 2, Flow 6). Outside those three, skip it — which is why it is P1.

Three screens called “Inbox”What it holdsUse it for
Inbox (left menu)180 rows, every conversation. No category rail, no read/handled statereading a thread, not triage

To get what
Turns an answer into a next step, and a “no” into a permanent stop. Lowest volume in the guide, highest value per event.
Build Q1 on the list, not on the Inbox page
The Inbox page has no notion of handled, so 180 rows tell you nothing about what is waiting. In Conversation : Received a reply on the list gives the actual 7.
Why 4.4 outranks 4.3
Answering badly costs one contact. Labelling badly costs every future send — mislabel an opt-out and campaign steps 2 and 3 still go out.

5Flow 4b · Schedule a call

L1  4b.1  From an interested reply           P0
L1  4b.2  Propose / confirm the time         P0
L1  4b.3  Set lifecycle to Call Scheduled    P0   otherwise sending continues
To get what
The finish line of the loop. Rarest step, and the reason the other four exist.
Why 4b.3 is not paperwork
The meeting is in a calendar; the campaign does not know until the lifecycle says so. Skip it and someone with a Thursday meeting still gets step 3 on Wednesday.

To get what
One hour of setup reaches a hundred people over two weeks. Highest return per hour an SDR has.
Build once, watch daily
6.1–6.6 happen once. 6.8 and 6.9 happen every day afterwards and are Flow 4 wearing another hat — same act, replies scoped to one campaign.
6.5 is the last free mistake
After hand-over the emails are real, no recall. And 6.5.5 has a consequence people miss: an unreviewed contact is simply not sent to, silently. A half-reviewed campaign under-sends and nothing on screen explains the gap.

Tier 3 · foundation and lookup

8Flow 1 · Log in & find your workspace

Read once on day one. Lowest reading value, highest blast radius — the clearest proof that depth in the tree is not the priority score. Reach × blast radius is.

9Flow 8 · Using filters

To get what
Every “who do I work on next” question is a filter. Two of them — In Conversation and Last Email Sent Date — are what make the daily queues possible, which promotes them to P0 even though the guide currently files them as ordinary filters.
No critical path of its own
It inherits the priority of whatever flow is using it, so a filter defect is never below P1.
