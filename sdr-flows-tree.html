SDR Guideline — Overview

Where the day actually goes. The ten flows in the order an SDR meets them at work, not the order they were written, each one with the part that cannot be routed around and the reason it exists.

Draft v2 · overview page for SDR Guideline. Detail stays in the guide; this page is the map.

Q1Which flow does an SDR care about most?

Flow 4 · View & answer replies. The only flow where work turns into an outcome. Everything before it is preparation: a list is not a reply, a send is not a reply.

Highest leverage per hour is a different flow: 6 · Manage a campaign. An hour there reaches a hundred people, an hour in Flow 3 reaches ten. Care and volume are separate questions.

Q2Which flow is traversed most?

Not a flow — a loop of four, run several times a day:

2 Find a contact  →  3 Send 1:1  →  4 Read & answer replies  →  5 Follow up
                      ↑                                            │
                      └────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Flow 8 · Using filters is touched as often but never alone: it runs inside 2, 6 and 7. That is why one broken filter reads as three broken flows.

Q3What decides what to read, and what to fix, first?

The critical path — nodes every reader passes and cannot route around, marked P0. A defect there stops the flow. P1 is common with a way around it, P2 genuinely rare. Depth in the tree does not set priority: reach × blast radius does. Log in is the shallowest node in the guide and blocks all ten flows.

Tier 1 · the daily loop — read these first

1Flow 4 · View & answer replies

L1  4.1  Open the Inbox                      P0   every session starts here
L1  4.2  Read the reply and its chip         P0   the chip is a reading, not a decision
L1  4.3  Answer it                           P0
L1  4.4  Set the lifecycle                   P0   ← the part that matters most
     └ hot reply → book the call (4b)        P1
     └ out of office → leave it              P1
     └ bounced → reach another way           P2
To get what
Turns an answer into a next step, and turns a “no” into a permanent stop.
When
Every time a reply lands. Not optional, not batched.
Why 4.4 outranks 4.3
Answering badly costs one contact. Labelling badly costs every future send — mislabel an opt-out and campaign steps 2 and 3 still go out to them.

2Flow 2 · Find a target contact

L1  2.1  Open your assigned list             P0
     └ list not assigned to you              P2
L1  2.2  View the contacts                   P0
     ├ Filter                                P0   ← the part that matters most
     ├ Sort                                  P1
     └ Change the view                       P2
L1  2.3  Pick & read a contact               P0
     ├ View company detail                   P1
     └ “+N” more roles                       P2
To get what
Decides who gets contacted today — the single decision the rest of the day inherits.
Widest blast radius in the guide
The filter runs inside Flow 2, Flow 6 and Flow 7.
Sort — why only Country and Last contact
Only those two set an order of work. Last contact turns the list into a queue so nobody at the bottom of page 8 is forgotten — the backbone of Flow 5. Country groups by timezone and language, for sending in local-hour batches and calling at a sane time. Title or company sorted A→Z leads to no decision, which is why they are absent. Not a gap.
View company detail — when it is actually needed
Three cases only. Before a 1:1, to find one true personal line (Flow 3). On a vague reply, to see who this is and whether a deal is live (Flow 4). While targeting, to check sponsor/portfolio or an existing client, so nobody gets pitched twice (Flow 2, Flow 6). Outside those three, skip it — which is why it is P1, not P0.

3Flow 3 · Send a 1:1 email

L1  3.1  Get into the composer               P0
     ├ 3.1.1  from the list row ✉            P0   ← carries almost all traffic
     ├ 3.1.2  from the contact profile       P1
     ├ 3.1.3  from Conversations             P1   history first
     └ 3.1.4  from a signal                  P2   draft written for you
L1  3.2  Write it                            P0
L1  3.3  Send                                P0
     └ WhatsApp / LinkedIn instead           P2
To get what
The touch that starts a conversation. Everything upstream exists to make this email worth opening.
Four doors, one room
All four reach the same composer; one carries the traffic. Read 3.1.1 as the path and the rest as alternatives — presenting four equal options to someone who just wants to write an email is the slowest way to explain it.
When to use 3.1.3 instead
When the contact has history. Opening from Conversations means you read what was said before you write, which is the difference between a follow-up and a duplicate.

4Flow 5 · Follow up on silent prospects

L1  5.1  Find who never answered             P0   ← a filter, again
L1  5.2  Decide it is worth a second touch   P0
L1  5.3  Send the follow-up                  P0   Flow 3 from here
To get what
Most replies come from the second touch, not the first. This flow is where the majority of the pipeline is actually created.
The whole flow is one query
Filter plus Flow 3. If “no reply yet” is wrong, the SDR either chases people who already answered — which reads as careless — or misses the ones who never did.

5Flow 4b · Schedule a call

L1  4b.1  From an interested reply           P0
L1  4b.2  Propose / confirm the time         P0
L1  4b.3  Set the lifecycle to Call Scheduled P0  ← otherwise sending continues
To get what
The finish line of the daily loop. Rarer than the rest, and the reason the rest exists.
Why 4b.3 is not paperwork
The meeting is in a calendar, but the campaign does not know until the lifecycle says so. Skip it and someone with a meeting on Thursday still gets step 3 on Wednesday.
Tier 2 · bulk work, in bursts

6Flow 6 · Manage a campaign

L1  6.1  Create it                           P0
L1  6.2  Build the sequence                  P0
     ├ A/B variants                          P2
     └ sidestep (conditional)                P2
L1  6.3  Read the list                       P1
L1  6.4  Add people                          P0
     └ 6.4b  Remove people                   P1
L1  6.5  Preview, personalize & review       P0   ← the gate
     ├ 6.5.3  skip a step                    P1
     ├ 6.5.4  personalize + save             P0
     ├ 6.5.5  mark done ✓                    P0   nothing sends without it
     └ clear out blocked contacts            P1
L1  6.6  Hand it over to be run              P0
L1  6.7  The Email Queue                     P1   admin-side
L1  6.8  Verify: Inbox & replies             P0
L1  6.9  Label the replies                   P0
L1  6.10 Finish the campaign                 P2   admin only
To get what
One hour of setup reaches a hundred people over two weeks. The highest return per hour an SDR has.
6.5 is the last free mistake
After the hand-over the emails are real and there is no recall.
6.5.5 has a mechanical consequence people miss
An unreviewed contact is simply not sent to, silently. A half-reviewed campaign under-sends and nothing on screen explains the gap.
Build vs watch
6.1–6.6 happen once. 6.8 and 6.9 happen every day afterwards, and are Flow 4 wearing a different hat.

7Flow 7 · Manage a Marketing Email

L1  7.1  Create it                           P0  ┐
L1  7.2  Write the template                  P0  │ build — once
L1  7.3  Add recipients                      P0  │
L1  7.4  Preview & review                    P0  ┘ ← the gate
L1  7.5  Hand over to Admin to send          P0
L1  7.6  The queue                           P1  ┐ watch — daily
L1  7.7  Read what came back                 P0  ┘
To get what
One send to a whole segment. No second step to correct course, no recall.
Why the gate matters more here than in Flow 6
A campaign has later steps to fix a bad first impression. A marketing email does not.
If you were handed a running one
Start at 7.7. The build half is not your job and reading it wastes the morning.
Tier 3 · foundation and lookup

8Flow 1 · Log in & find your workspace

L1  1.1  Log in                              P0   blocks all ten flows
L1  1.2  Find your way around                P1   read once, then never
To get what
Read once on day one. Lowest reading value in the guide, highest blast radius when broken — the clearest proof that depth is not priority.

9Flow 8 · Using filters

reference — runs inside 2 · 6 · 7, never walked end to end
L1  include ✓ / exclude ✕                    P0
L1  Lifecycles                               P0
L1  Has signal · keyword · countries · cities P1
L1  Simple vs Advanced                       P2
To get what
Every “who do I work on next” question in the product is a filter. It has no critical path of its own — it inherits the priority of whatever flow is using it, which is why a filter defect is never below P1.

10Flow 9 · FAQ & edge-case index

To get what
A lookup surface, not a flow. In the tree model it becomes an auto-generated index of every branch node sorted by priority, each showing its parent — so a stuck reader lands next to the step they were on, not in a list of 144 unrelated cases.
